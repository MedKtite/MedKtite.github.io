CASE STUDY / PRODUCT DESIGN & DEVELOPMENT

Ancora — Making US LLC formation understandable from Morocco

From choosing a state to following a filing: bringing the questions, documents and next steps into one service.

Angular · Spring Boot · PostgreSQL / Live client platform

Before writing code, I studied the service

I started from two perspectives: the cost of delivering an LLC service and the experience of finding one as a founder in Casablanca. Those checks shaped Ancora’s scope, pricing and language.

01 / Compare the provider-side costs

I compared registered-agent and formation costs at Harbor Compliance, Bizee and Northwest for New Mexico and Wyoming. That comparison led me to focus on those two states and use a cost-plus pricing model.

02 / Search as a founder in Casablanca

I searched in French and English. The results I found were US-wide providers or Fiverr gigs. I did not find an offer that addressed the Office des Changes, the CIN or costs in dirhams.

What this changed in Ancora

I focused the service on Wyoming and New Mexico and built around Moroccan founders: prices in dirhams, guidance in familiar languages and document collection that accommodates the CIN. The product also separates formation from banking assistance, so founders can see what the service handles and what still depends on a provider’s decision.

01 / Explain what is available after payment

Difficulty

A founder who has paid may expect every document immediately. An empty library needs to explain whether something is missing or the filing has not reached that stage yet.

Solution

Give official documents a dedicated place and explain when they will appear. The signed-in library names the Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement and EIN letter, and states that they become available once ready.

Decision and trade-off

A clear empty state sets expectations before the first document is published. It still needs to stay consistent with actual fulfillment and document-publication rules.

02 / Explain what is happening while people wait

Difficulty

State filing, an EIN request and banking review have different owners and timelines. A single “in progress” label cannot tell the founder whether to act or wait.

Solution

Show dated activity, available documents and the next action. Internally, track individual fulfillment tasks and give paused cases a reason. Keep bank application review separate from the LLC’s formation status.

Decision and trade-off

More detailed states require consistent rules in the back office. The benefit depends on operators recording progress and missing information promptly.

03 / Give questions a clear place to go

Difficulty

A founder can get stuck on a state choice, a document request or a banking question. If each answer lives in a separate email, it is harder to connect the question to the case.

Solution

Provide self-service explanations and a visible contact route. The client workspace organizes support requests by topic; the operations team has an assignee, conversation history and a request status. Email notifications bring the founder back when action is needed.

Decision and trade-off

Human support remains necessary for exceptions. Structured requests help preserve context, but they still need clear ownership and timely replies.

04 / Avoid a paid dead end

Difficulty

A formation package may include banking assistance that is unavailable for an owner’s residence. Discovering this after checkout creates rework and a difficult support conversation.

Solution

Ask for the country of residence before payment and refuse packs that include an account the owner cannot open. If a bank declines the application, keep the case open until an alternative or refund is handled. After five business days without an answer, remind the client and the team. The banking provider still makes the approval decision.

Expected benefit

Earlier eligibility checks should reduce avoidable paid cases and refund conversations. That is a product hypothesis, not a measured result.

Engineering the handoffs

Angular connects the public site, founder dashboard and internal tools. Spring Boot owns the application workflow, permissions and payment logic, with PostgreSQL storing the shared records.

Keep rules behind the interface

Eligibility checks happen at submission and checkout. Fulfillment tracks each pack item separately. Document review and publication are distinct steps, so an uploaded file does not automatically become a client deliverable.

Design for the work after submission

Operations can request corrections, pause a case with a reason and track assisted account opening. A provider decline keeps the task open until the team handles an alternative or refund.

What I would validate next

The portfolio currently describes Ancora as preparing for launch. This case study presents the implementation and its design rationale; measured user outcomes are still to be established.

Application clarity

Observe whether founders choose an eligible pack, complete member details and recover from validation errors. Track completion, abandonment by step and requests for help.

Operational clarity

Ask an operator to identify a blocked case, explain the blocker and resolve a document correction. Measure time to the next useful action and unnecessary handoffs.

Ongoing compliance

Check whether founders can distinguish a filing handled by Ancora, an automatic renewal and a reminder-only obligation. Those responsibilities should be clear before a deadline arrives.

What I learned

Building across the client and operations interfaces makes the dependencies visible. A useful progress tracker needs the same definitions as the fulfillment workflow. A clear dashboard starts with clear ownership in the underlying service.
